How we work with restaurant groups

Agents in the systems, people on the floor

A swarm of agents reads every POS export, invoice, schedule, menu and review at a scale no consultant can match. Our forward-deployed team does what agents cannot: the interviews, the service walks, the access and the judgment that turn a group's knowledge into decisions its partners can act on.

How an engagement runs

Five phases, with a working picture of the group by the end of the second week

Each phase ends with something the partners and the operations team can use, so value shows up long before the engagement ends.

Days 1 to 2
01 · Days 1 to 2

Scope and access

We agree the questions that matter most, whether that is a pending acquisition, prime cost, a struggling location or the events pipeline, set access levels, connect the first systems and book time with the operations leaders, chefs and GMs around service.

You get: a scoped question list, an access plan and an interview calendar that respects service.

02 · Days 3 to 7

Capture

Agents read the POS exports, invoices, schedules, payroll, menus, recipes, leases and reviews. Our team runs the interviews and works lunch, dinner and the close. Every new fact arrives with its source, and a person reviews it before it counts.

You get: a working brain of the group, searchable by your team.

03 · Days 8 to 10

Diagnose and read out

We rank locations on four-wall economics, find where prime cost leaks between the invoice and the plate, map key-person risk and the events and catering pipeline, size each opportunity by value, confidence and effort, and walk the partners and operations leadership through what we found.

You get: a ranked location view, an opportunity list and a risk register, every item cited, and a readout with the partners.

04 · Weeks 3 to 12

Act

The plan moves into the brain with owners and dates. Persistent agents go to work on the journeys you choose, such as event inquiries or corporate accounts, track progress, flag drift early and draft the weekly readout. We help the operations team land the first wins.

You get: a live plan, weekly readouts and the first wins delivered.

05 · Ongoing

Review and measure

On subscription, every shift report, invoice, meeting and decision keeps feeding the brain, every new location and acquisition is absorbed into it, and we measure what it delivers every quarter, so the group's knowledge grows instead of walking out of the door with each departure.

You get: an operating brain that supports every opening, acquisition and the eventual sale, and a quarterly review for the partners.

Our approach

Questions we hear

What partners ask before they start

Which systems do you work with?

The POS, inventory, scheduling, payroll, accounting, reservation and event systems the group already runs, including groups that run several POS systems after acquisitions. If a system exports data, agents can read it; where it offers an API, agents keep the brain current from it.

How much time does it take from our chefs and GMs?

Expect about an hour with each operations leader, chef and GM, scheduled around service, shorter conversations with the people who run key stations, and a thirty-minute weekly readout for the partners. Agents and our team do the rest.

We run several concepts and brands. Does that work?

Yes. The group has one brain, and each concept and location is its own part of it, so you can compare them on the same terms while each keeps what makes it different. Access follows roles, so a GM sees their location and the partners see the group.

Will agents talk to our guests and clients?

Only on the journeys you choose, such as event inquiries or corporate accounts, and within rules you set: what can be offered, what needs a person's approval and when a person takes over. Guests and clients are told when they are dealing with an assistant, and every message is recorded with the facts it relied on.

Who owns the brain?

You do. The group's brain runs in its own instance, can be exported in open formats at any time, and transfers with the business when you sell, so a buyer inherits it running.

Does this replace our operations team or our consultants?

It changes what they spend their time on. The brain does the reading, the reconciling and the chasing, so operations leaders and advisers spend their time on the floor, on people and on decisions, and they can work from the brain too.

What do you need from us to start a pilot?

A partner who sponsors it, an operations leader who owns it day to day, access to the first systems and documents, and time with the chefs and GMs in the first week.
