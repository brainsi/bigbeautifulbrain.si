What we do for restaurant groups

We work where the group needs the understanding most

Restaurant and hospitality groups bring us in at the moments when knowing how a venue really works decides the outcome: before a concept joins the group, in its first ninety days, every week of service, when a lease comes up, when an event inquiry lands, and ahead of a recapitalization or sale.

Acquiring a concept or a group · 2 to 3 weeks before signing

See what each venue really earns before you sign

We load the seller's P&Ls, POS exports, invoices, payroll, leases and anything else they can share into a brain, then map how each venue actually makes its money: covers, average check, menu mix, prime cost, occupancy and the people who hold it together. You see where the asking price rests on evidence and where it rests on the seller's story, and what to ask before the price is set.

  • Four-wall economics for every venue, with sources
  • Lease terms, renewals, assignments and landlord consents in one view
  • The key people, recipes and suppliers the business depends on
  • The questions to put to the seller before you sign

The first 90 days · after the concept joins the group

Integrate a concept without losing what made it work

The first weeks after a concept joins the group decide whether the regulars stay. We interview the chef, the GM, the bar lead and the people on the line, capture recipes, prep lists, opening and closing routines, supplier terms and the quirks of every system, and turn what we find into a ninety-day plan with owners and dates. Hiring, firing and promotion decisions are made against evidence rather than first impressions.

  • Recipes, specs and routines captured before anyone leaves
  • Every supplier, contract and system mapped onto the group's own
  • People decisions made with evidence: who holds the place together
  • A 90-day plan with weekly readouts for the partners

Every venue, one picture · every week

Run every venue from one source of truth

Groups that grow by acquisition end up with different POS systems, different inventory habits and different ways of counting labor. The brain reads across all of them, so prime cost, labor, waste, comps, voids, reviews and guest feedback are compared venue by venue and concept by concept, every number traced to its source and every variance explained by the people who run the floor.

  • Prime cost and labor by venue, daypart and concept
  • Theoretical against actual food cost, with the leaks named
  • Menu engineering: what sells, what earns and what to cut
  • Reviews and guest feedback tied to the shift, the menu and the team

Portfolio optimization · when the decision comes

Remodel, convert, relocate or close on venue-level evidence

Most groups carry venues that quietly lose money once occupancy and labor are counted, and every development pipeline depends on choosing the next site well, whether a ground-up build or second-generation space. We rank venues on four-wall margin, comps and lease events, show what the winners have in common, and model what happens to the group if a venue is remodeled, relocated, converted to another concept or closed.

  • A ranked view of every venue on four-wall economics
  • Lease events, renewals and exit costs on one calendar
  • An opening playbook drawn from your flagship and best openings, applied to the next
  • Closures planned so staff, guests and suppliers move with you

Events, catering and regulars · all year

Grow the revenue that compounds

Private dining, events, catering and corporate accounts carry some of the highest-value revenue a group earns, and they are won or lost on follow-through. Persistent agents answer inquiries in minutes, keep every booking moving through proposals, deposits, tastings and headcounts, brief the kitchen and the floor, and come back to each client before their next occasion. Regulars and VIPs are recognized at every venue in the group, not only the one where the bartender knows them.

  • Every inquiry answered in minutes and every booking followed through
  • Corporate and catering accounts kept warm between events
  • Regulars and VIPs recognized across every venue
  • Rebooking and repeat visits measured every month

People and know-how · all the time

Keep what your best people know

People in restaurants and hotels quit at about twice the rate of the average US industry (4.2% a month in 2025 against 2.0%, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics), and every departure takes knowledge with it. The brain keeps the recipes, the routines, the supplier relationships and the reasons behind decisions, so a new GM or chef is productive in days, standards hold across venues, and the group can see who its next leaders and working partners are.

  • Onboarding for new managers and chefs, drawn from the brain
  • Training and standards that hold across venues
  • Key-person risk named, with a successor in view
  • Hiring and promotion decisions made on evidence

The next transaction · 6 to 10 weeks before launch

Go to market with the evidence already organized

Sponsors and strategic acquirers price AUVs, four-wall margins, comps, lease terms, the depth of the management team and how much of the group lives in documented systems rather than in the founder's head. Whether you bring in a capital partner, recapitalize, sell a majority stake or divest a concept, the brain holds all of it with its sources, so the equity story, the data room and the buyer's diligence come together in weeks, and the new partner inherits a group that keeps running.

  • Unit economics and same-store history a buyer can verify
  • Leases, licenses and supplier contracts organized
  • Documented recipes, standards and processes
  • A brain the buyer keeps using from day one

On the floor and in the systems

Agents read the numbers, and our people work the service

Much of what makes a restaurant work is never written down. Our team goes and gets it, so the brain reflects how the group really runs rather than how the manual says it runs.

Interviews

Conversations with partners, operations leaders, chefs, GMs and the people on the line, recorded and filed into the brain with consent.

Service walks

We watch lunch, dinner and the close, from the back door to the pass to the host stand, because the gap between the manual and the shift lives there.

System connections

POS, inventory, scheduling, payroll, accounting, reservations and events, including groups running several POS systems after acquisitions.

Data discovery

Invoices, menus, recipes, schedules and reviews pulled together and reconciled, so the numbers agree before anyone acts on them.
