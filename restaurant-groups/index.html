What we do

We give a new owner the understanding of a restaurant that usually takes a year, in the first weeks.

Every restaurant you buy arrives with its own recipes, suppliers, systems, habits and people, and most of what makes it work is in the heads of the chef, the general manager and the bar lead. When one of them leaves, it leaves too.

We capture that knowledge once, into a brain of the whole group in which every fact traces back to its source: the POS, the invoices, the schedules, the leases, the reviews and the people who run the floor. You see every location in one place within weeks, decide with evidence instead of instinct, and keep that understanding working for the partners, the operations team and every GM.

See every location as it really is

Four-wall economics, menus, people and suppliers, location by location and concept by concept, with every number traced to its source.

Find the margin while you can act

Prime cost leaks, labor that does not match the covers, menu items that sell but do not earn, and locations that quietly lose money, sized and ranked.

Keep it working every service

Agents keep the picture current from every shift, invoice and booking, follow up every event and account, and report what changed. We measure what it delivers every quarter.

Persistent agents in hospitality

Agents that stay with the guests and clients who carry the revenue

The most valuable relationships in hospitality run for months or years: a wedding booked a year ahead, a company that holds its holiday party with you every December, a regular who brings clients to three of your venues. Each one can be lost to a slow reply or a dropped follow-up.

Our agents stay with each of these journeys. They answer in minutes, remember every detail, brief the kitchen and the floor, come back before the next occasion and hand to your team whenever judgment or a personal touch is needed.

AI in restaurants today

Good at single tasks, short on the context of the whole group

The largest chains have built their own platforms and spent years unifying the data underneath before trusting AI with guests. Most groups run a different tool for each task, on data that does not agree, while the know-how that ties it together sits with a few people.

26%

of US operators say their restaurants use tools that incorporate AI, and only 6% use AI to take customer orders.

National Restaurant Association, State of the Restaurant Industry 2026
< 2%

of 2,145 drive-thru mystery-shop visits in 2026 had voice AI take the order, after years of announcements.

Intouch Insight, 26th annual drive-thru study, October 2026
1in 5

operators name data silos between their vendors as one of their biggest technology problems for 2026.

Informa Foodservice, 2026 Restaurant Technology Outlook, via Nation's Restaurant News
55%

of operators train staff mainly shoulder to shoulder. Only 10% rely on an employee handbook, so the know-how lives in people.

Restaurant365, 2026 mid-year industry report (operator survey)

Where AI already helps

Point tools that each do one job

  • Answering the phone and taking reservations, including after hours
  • Drafting replies to event inquiries for a person to send
  • Forecasting covers and building schedules
  • Reading invoices and tracking food cost
  • Hiring screens and interview scheduling
  • Voice ordering at the drive-thru, still rare in practice

What is still missing

An understanding of the group, kept working

  • One picture across POS, inventory, scheduling, payroll and booking systems that do not agree
  • What the chefs and GMs know, which no tool captures and which leaves when they do
  • Context: each tool knows its task, and none knows the business
  • Follow-through on journeys that last months, not one conversation
  • An owner's view of what each location really earns, and what to do about it
  • A record a lender or a buyer can check

How an engagement runs

From kickoff to a working picture of the group in two weeks

Agents read the POS, the invoices, the schedules and the reviews. Our team does the interviews, works the service and does the heavy lifting that turns access into understanding.

Days 1 to 2
01 · Days 1 to 2

Scope and access

We agree the questions that matter most, connect the POS, inventory, scheduling, accounting and booking systems, and book time with the operations leaders, chefs and GMs around service.

02 · Days 3 to 7

Capture

Agents read every export, invoice, menu, lease and review while our team runs the interviews and works lunch, dinner and the close. Every fact arrives with its source, and a person reviews it before it counts.

03 · Days 8 to 10

Diagnose and read out

We rank locations on four-wall economics, find where prime cost leaks, map key-person risk and the events pipeline, and walk the partners through what we found. A working picture of the group lands in two weeks.

04 · Weeks 3 to 12

Act

The plan lives in the brain with owners and dates. Persistent agents go to work on the journeys you choose, track progress, flag drift and draft the weekly readout.

05 · Ongoing

Review and measure

Every shift, invoice and booking keeps feeding the brain, every new location is absorbed into it, and we measure what it delivers every quarter, through to the sale.
